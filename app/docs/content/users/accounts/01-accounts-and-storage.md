A Space is a container for one project in Spacewave, and every Space is stored
somewhere. You can keep your Spaces in three places. They differ in what
survives if something goes wrong.

## In this browser

You do not need an account. Choose **Continue without account**, and Spacewave
stores your Spaces in the browser you are using. There is nothing to set up, it
works offline, and it is the quickest way to try Spacewave.

This storage belongs to the browser. Browsers clear their own storage when disk
space runs low, and they do not ask first. Use it to try things, not to keep
them.

## On this computer

The desktop app keeps your Spaces in a folder on your own disk. You can back up
that folder like any other. If you have used Spacewave on this computer before,
the desktop app can open that existing folder.

Choose this when you want your data on your own hardware, covered by your own
backups.

## In Spacewave Cloud

Spacewave Cloud is Spacewave's hosted service. A Cloud account adds sync
between devices, backup, and shared Spaces, so you can continue on your phone
what you started on your laptop. Your content is encrypted, and we cannot read
it.

You can sign in with a password, a passkey, your organization's single sign-on,
or a backup key. Cloud accounts also have a recovery email and billing.

## Which to choose

Try Spacewave in the browser. When your work starts to matter, move it to
Cloud, or to the desktop app if you would rather keep the disk yourself. You
are not locked in. [Move to Cloud](/docs/users/accounts/move-to-cloud) explains
how to move Spaces later.
