Two settings protect your account. A backup key gets you back into your account
if you lose your password or your device. A lock decides whether someone who
picks up your device can open your Spaces.

A backup key is a key, not a copy of your files. Having one does not mean your
Spaces are backed up.

## Get a backup key

Open **Crypto & Keys** in settings and download a backup key. With a Spacewave
Cloud account, you confirm who you are first.

The downloaded file is your way back in. Keep it somewhere other than the
device that made it: a password manager, another computer, or a USB drive. A
backup key kept on the device it recovers does not help when you lose that
device.

With the backup key you can sign in on a new device, add another way to sign
in, or reset a forgotten PIN.

## Choose how it locks

**Auto-unlock** opens Spacewave as soon as you open the app. It is convenient,
but anyone who can open your device can open your Spaces.

**PIN lock** asks for a PIN each time. It takes a moment longer and protects
your Spaces from anyone who does not know the PIN.

You can switch between the two, or change your PIN, in settings.

If you forget your PIN, choose **Forgot PIN?** and sign in again with your
account password or your backup key. An account on this device that has
neither cannot reset its PIN this way.

## Before it matters

Do these three things once:

1. Download a backup key and store it somewhere else.
2. Choose auto-unlock or PIN lock on purpose.
3. Keep anything you want to keep in Spacewave Cloud or the desktop app.

Cloud backs up your Spaces for you. Work kept only in a browser can still be
deleted by that browser.
